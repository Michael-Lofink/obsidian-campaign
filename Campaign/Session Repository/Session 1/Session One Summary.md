# Session One Summary Sheet

## “My Little Village”

### Session at a Glance

Gib began the session living a precarious yet increasingly comfortable life in the small Mwangi town of Neeb. He went to work, interacted with the people he had slowly begun trusting, and accepted a routine delivery for the reclusive blacksmith Tilda. That errand exposed him to a murder, a hidden child, and two kholo searching Tilda’s home.

Gib attempted to save the child by drawing the killers away. He escaped them through the wilderness and returned within sight of Neeb, only to discover the settlement under attack. Among the raiders stood Molak, watching Gib’s home burn. Molak looked directly toward Gib despite the immense distance between them.

---

# Opening Vision: Molak

The session opened from Molak’s perspective.

Molak had destroyed an entire village while searching for someone who had taken something from him. Standing amid burning homes and severed bodies, he looked into a river and found his own reflection increasingly alien. Numerous voices spoke within him, insisting that they knew where “she” and “he” were and demanding that Molak listen.

This sequence established:

- Molak is actively searching for at least one person and a stolen object.
    
- He is massacring settlements during that search.
    
- His body or consciousness contains multiple voices.
    
- His original identity appears to have been that of a dromaar or orc-adjacent humanoid.
    
- His reflection no longer feels like his own.
    
- He is already geographically close enough to threaten Gib’s life directly.
    

The opening also created a deliberate parallel between Molak and Gib. Both are beings whose reflected identities have become separated from the lives and bodies from which they originated.

---

# Scene One: Gib’s Morning

Gib awoke in his daub-and-thatch hut after his hired knocker-upper, Bill, pounded on the door.

His home was established as:

- A small hut built against a large stone outside Neeb.
    
- Secured by several improvised “deadbolts.”
    
- Filled with apparently disorganized treasures whose locations Gib knows precisely.
    
- Furnished with a cheap thatch bed and pillow.
    
- Shared with a cow and chicken, whom Gib regards as pets rather than food.
    
- The place where Gib sleeps with and protects his mirror.
    

Gib initially reacted to the knocking with fear, instinctively clutching his mirror. After remembering that he had hired Bill, he checked the exterior carefully before preparing for work.

He ate ticks and insects from his livestock as breakfast, gently interacting with the cow before leaving.

### Characterization Established

Gib is highly paranoid and security-conscious. His fear is grounded in experience rather than presented as irrational. He also shows tenderness toward animals and assigns value to objects others would dismiss.

His mirror remains his most important possession and his immediate source of comfort.

---

# Scene Two: The Day Laborers Association

Gib arrived at the Day Laborers Association only slightly late. The union leader, Terrell, confronted him because Gib was already on probation for previous tardiness.

Terrell explained that Neeb’s defenses were being reinforced because of reports involving:

- Raiders in neighboring areas.
    
- Missing sheep.
    
- Theft and other disturbances.
    
- Suspicion directed toward nearby goblins and kholo.
    

Gib’s anxiety increased upon hearing about the raids.

### Bill and the Broken Pocket Watch

Bill was waiting near Gib’s equipment. He appeared tired and somewhat nervous. He offered Gib a damaged metallic trinket because he knew Gib liked odd objects.

The object was identified as a broken pocket watch:

- Bronze or similarly colored metal with a golden sheen.
    
- Exposed internal gears and hands.
    
- Missing its protective glass.
    
- Apparently foreign to Neeb.
    
- Not obviously magical.
    

Bill placed it on the ground rather than handing it directly to Gib, revealing a degree of discomfort or prejudice toward physical contact with him. Gib recognized the behavior and chose not to confront it.

Gib accepted the pocket watch for further investigation.

### Work Minigame

Gib spent the day producing mud bricks and reinforcing Neeb’s wall. The labor minigame resulted in:

**Payment earned: 6 gp**

The wall Gib helped construct became significant later in the session when the raiders broke through it.

---

# Scene Three: Terrell’s Delivery

After the workday, Terrell approached Gib with a personal favor.

Terrell had argued with Tilda, an elderly and reclusive blacksmith who lived outside town. Tilda had forbidden him from returning to her property. He therefore asked Gib to deliver a wrapped weapon for repair.

Gib briefly felt an impulse inherited from Big to demand payment. He recognized the impulse as belonging more to Big’s habits than his own and deliberately rejected it.

He agreed to deliver the weapon without compensation.

### Important Character Moment

This was one of the clearest demonstrations of Gib differentiating himself from Big.

Gib possesses Big’s memories and instincts, though he does not automatically accept them as his own. He can identify an inherited impulse and consciously decide what kind of person he wishes to become.

Terrell showed some genuine approval of Gib’s work and implied that Gib might have earned a more secure place within the association.

---

# Scene Four: Tilda’s Homestead

Gib traveled beyond Neeb and into the forest toward Tilda’s isolated forge.

The homestead stood within a deliberately cleared circle of forest. Tilda had apparently removed nearby trees to reduce the danger of starting another fire.

Upon arriving, Gib noticed that the forge appeared unusually quiet. He conducted a cautious perimeter search rather than approaching openly.

### The Two Kholo

Gib observed two gray-furred figures speaking near the entrance. They appeared to be kholo:

- One was leaner.
    
- One was broader and better armed.
    
- At least one had blood on his muzzle.
    
- They were searching for someone they believed should be at the homestead.
    
- Their “intel” had told them their target was there.
    
- Their casual conversation suggested little concern over the violence they had committed.
    

The kholo had not yet detected Gib.

---

# Scene Five: Tilda’s Death

Gib approached the rear wall and peered through cracks in the timber.

Inside, he saw:

- Tilda lying dead in a pool of blood.
    
- A crude child’s drawing of a tall figure and a smaller figure holding hands.
    
- Small bloody tracks leading toward a dark wood-storage recess near the dining area.
    
- An open front door providing the home’s only significant light.
    

The small tracks implied that a child had walked or crawled through Tilda’s blood and hidden inside the house.

### The Wrapped Weapon

Realizing he was in danger, Gib opened Terrell’s package.

The weapon was a finesse weapon with the following traits:

- Agile
    
- Finesse
    
- Deadly d8
    

The precise weapon type should be recorded from its item card if this summary is added to the campaign archive.

---

# Scene Six: Entering Through the Mirror

Gib used his mirror ability to project himself into Tilda’s home through a visible interior space.

The manifestation was described through recursive reflections: Gib looked through the crack, angled the mirror toward the interior, and saw himself looking back from the position he intended to occupy.

This was the session’s first major demonstration of Gib’s supernatural nature.

### Limitation Confirmed

Gib can reposition himself through his reflection, though he cannot transport another creature with him using the same ability.

That limitation became crucial once he discovered the child.

---

# Scene Seven: The Hidden Child

Inside the house, Gib heard a quiet voice and discovered a young human child tugging at his clothing.

The child was:

- Approximately four or five years old.
    
- Dark-skinned with gray eyes.
    
- Covered in blood.
    
- Apparently unwounded.
    
- Suppressing his sobs to remain hidden.
    
- Almost certainly covered in Tilda’s blood.
    

The drawings inside the home suggest that Tilda may have been the child’s mother, guardian, or close caretaker.

### Gib’s Decision

Gib’s first instinct was to flee and leave the child behind. He then recognized that the child would likely die from the killers, exposure, or starvation.

Gib instructed the child to:

1. Remain hidden until the kholo left.
    
2. Run toward Neeb.
    
3. Be strong and run for his life.
    

Gib could not take the child through the mirror and did not risk leading him through the occupied front entrance.

This choice preserved Gib’s fear and self-protective instincts while showing that he is unwilling to knowingly abandon a vulnerable person to certain death.

### Child’s Status

**Unknown.**

The child was never seen reaching Neeb, and the subsequent raid may have placed him in even greater danger.

---

# Scene Eight: The Diversion

Gib exited the house through his mirror and returned to the forest line.

He attempted to create an auditory diversion to lure the kholo away from the house and give the child an opportunity to escape.

The Deception check failed against a Perception DC of 20.

The kholo located Gib and pursued him into the forest.

Despite the failure, the diversion may still have accomplished part of Gib’s goal. Both kholo left the immediate vicinity of the house to chase him, potentially creating an opening for the child.

---

# Scene Nine: Wilderness Chase

Gib fled through the same terrain he had crossed on the way to Tilda’s forge.

The chase used a sequence of obstacle cards. Gib needed at least 10 Chase Points before exhausting the obstacle deck.

### Obstacles Encountered

|Obstacle|Approach|Result|
|---|---|---|
|Rushing river|Survival|Success|
|Quicksand|Perception|Success|
|Small ravine|Acrobatics|Success|
|Wasps|Survival|Critical success|
|Startled deer|Nature|Critical success|
|Steep hills|Perception|Critical failure|
|Dense forest|Survival|Failure|
|Fallen tree or narrow crossing|Acrobatics|Success|
|Deep mud|Perception|Critical success|
|Damaged bridge|Crafting|Failure|

Gib finished with exactly:

**10 Chase Points**

He escaped the kholo.

### Chase Rules Used

- Success generated the Chase Points printed for the selected skill.
    
- Critical success granted a +2 circumstance bonus to the next obstacle.
    
- Critical failure imposed a –2 circumstance penalty on the next obstacle.
    
- Circumstance bonuses and penalties followed PF2e stacking rules.
    

The chase succeeded by the narrowest possible margin, reinforcing the sense that Gib survived through a combination of awareness, adaptability, and desperation.

---

# Scene Ten: The Destruction of Neeb

After escaping, Gib reached an overlook and looked back toward town.

Neeb was burning.

Mounted raiders had broken through the newly built wall and were massacring the population. Homes were on fire, families were fleeing, and townspeople were trying unsuccessfully to fight the flames and attackers.

Gib witnessed Bill attempting to help extinguish the fires.

A mounted attacker decapitated him.

### Confirmed Casualty

**Bill — dead.**

Bill’s death carried additional weight because he had:

- Woken Gib that morning.
    
- Given him the broken pocket watch.
    
- Accepted Gib’s awkward attempt at friendship.
    
- Begun to form part of Gib’s newly established community.
    

---

# Final Revelation: Molak

Gib saw his own home burning.

Standing outside it was Molak.

Molak was identifiable by:

- His large build.
    
- A blue-white snake tattoo extending from his back down his arm.
    
- A large, dull-looking blade.
    
- His direct connection to Gib’s fragmented memories.
    

Molak turned and appeared to look directly into Gib’s eyes across an impossible distance.

The session ended on this moment.

### Implications

Molak may have been able to sense Gib through their shared reflective origin. His presence outside Gib’s home suggests the attack on Neeb may have had a specific purpose beyond ordinary raiding.

Possible motivations include:

- Molak was searching for Gib.
    
- Molak was searching for Gib’s mirror.
    
- Molak was following a connection to Sivanah or the Mirror World.
    
- The raid was used to flush Gib out or destroy his attachments.
    
- Molak’s internal voices directed him specifically to Neeb.
    

---

# Character Development: Gib

## Beginning of the Session

Gib had started to establish a life:

- A home of his own.
    
- Pets he cared for.
    
- Steady employment.
    
- A cautious friendship with Bill.
    
- Growing acceptance among the laborers.
    
- A familiar routine within Neeb.
    

## Decisions That Defined Him

Gib deliberately resisted Big’s impulse to exploit Terrell’s favor for money.

He entered a murder scene despite his fear.

He chose to help the child after seriously considering abandoning him.

He drew the kholo away despite knowing the danger.

He survived through caution and cleverness rather than direct violence.

## End of the Session

Everything giving Gib a sense of stability was attacked simultaneously:

- His home was burned.
    
- His workplace and labor were destroyed.
    
- Bill was killed.
    
- Tilda was murdered.
    
- The hidden child’s fate remained uncertain.
    
- Molak found him.
    

Session One therefore functions as the destruction of Gib’s temporary ordinary life and the beginning of his direct conflict with Molak.

# Open Mysteries and Active Hooks

## Molak’s Search

Who is Molak searching for, and what was taken from him?

The opening references both “she” and “he,” suggesting multiple targets or identities.

## Molak’s Voices

Who or what are the consciousnesses speaking inside Molak?

Their language implies shared knowledge and a degree of collective intent.

## The Impossible Eye Contact

How did Molak see Gib from such a great distance?

This may reveal a sensory or metaphysical connection between mirrored beings.

## The Raid on Neeb

Was the attack a conventional raid, a targeted assault arranged by Molak, or both?

## Tilda’s Murder

Why were the kholo searching her home?

They expected a male target to be present. Their target may have been the child, Terrell, Gib, or another unknown figure.

## The Child

Who is he, and what is his connection to Tilda?

Did he follow Gib’s instructions and escape before the raid?

## The Bloody Tracks

The tracks led from Tilda’s body to the child’s hiding place. This supports the interpretation that he witnessed her death and crossed through her blood.

## The Broken Pocket Watch

Where did Bill find it?

Its foreign origin may connect it to the raiders, Molak, a larger settlement, or a future plot thread.

## Terrell’s Weapon

Why was it dull, and what had Terrell truly been using it against?

His claims about goblins and wolves may have concealed other activity.

## Neeb’s Survivors

Terrell, Gib’s livestock, the hidden child, and most of the town remain unaccounted for.

# Session Themes

The session centered on identity, fragile belonging, and chosen morality.

Gib repeatedly confronted the difference between inherited instinct and personal choice. He resisted Big’s greed, overcame his instinct to abandon the child, and endangered himself to create an opportunity for someone else to survive.

The session also established that Gib’s developing individuality carries emotional consequences. His new attachments gave him reasons to act compassionately and gave Molak’s arrival the power to devastate him.

The final image completes the session’s central movement: Gib began the day protecting his small home from an ordinary knock and ended it watching Molak stand before that home as it burned.

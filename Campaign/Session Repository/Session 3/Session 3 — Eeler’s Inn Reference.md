# Encounter Guardrails

Back to: [[Session 3 — Eeler’s Inn Progression#Guardrails|Progression Guardrails]]

Gib is alone and 1st level.

The possessed rats should retreat from early encounters after taking enough damage, losing enough numbers, or failing their immediate objective. These early encounters should feel like skirmishes with the same infestation.

The possessed rat nest is the main rat encounter. Earlier combats should build toward the nest and make the final confrontation feel like the source of the infestation.

Avoid combining a damaging haunt with a full combat encounter unless Gib has a clear way to avoid, disable, or retreat from one of the threats.

Hollis can assist with barricades, repairs, warnings, and basic support. Gib should remain the person making the dangerous choices.

Eeler became a lich accidentally through the inn’s malfunctioning hospitality magic. His transformation did not require mass sacrifice. He is a victim of the haunting and becomes a helpful NPC if rescued.

The candlelight that drew Gib to the inn was supernatural. Hollis knows the traveler custom that one light means occupied, but he did not light the specific candle Gib saw. The inn lit it because it recognized that it was occupied and because Eeler’s weakened presence was trying to draw help.

The Seventh Witness does not gain Witnessed Echoes this session. Its reveal concerns the existing reflective-glass transformation.

# NPC Reference

## Hollis Venn

Back to: [[Session 3 — Eeler’s Inn Progression#Scene 2 Hollis’s Refuge|Scene 2]]

### Spotlight

Hollis is the trapped carpenter who has survived because he turns fear into procedures.

He is the first living proof that Eeler’s Inn can protect someone and endanger them at the same time. He gives Gib a human reason to care about reclaiming rooms, and he makes the barricade system feel practical.

### Fill

Hollis has been trapped in the entrance refuge for several days. He sleeps in short bursts with his back against furniture. He keeps a hammer in hand, marks safe boards with chalk, and moves lights whenever he shifts the barricade line.

He entered because he saw the candle and knew the road custom: one light means occupied. He later realized he had not found a normal refuge.

### Edge

Hollis solves problems by controlling space.

He wedges doors, braces tables, counts exits, tests floorboards, listens to walls, and turns every room into a structure that can hold or fail. When afraid, he does something physical and useful.

His flaw is that he can become too focused on holding the line. If a room is failing, his instinct is to seal it off before the danger spreads. This can create tension if Gib wants to take a risk to save something or someone.

### Practical

Hollis worked river roads, docks, hunting lodges, and small inns along the Vanji. He has repaired buildings after floods, storms, rot, and monster damage.

He carries a small tin of square nails from his mentor. He does not spend them casually. If he uses one on a door, he believes that door matters.

### Motivation

Hollis wants to leave the inn alive.

After Eeler is rescued, his motivation can shift. He wants to repair the inn because the candle drew him in, kept him alive, and brought Gib to the door. He does not fully trust the place, but he respects things that hold under pressure.

### Table Mannerisms

- Taps wood before trusting it.
    
- Marks safe doors with chalk.
    
- Measures danger in practical terms: “That door has one shove left in it.”
    
- Checks the candle whenever the room goes quiet.
    
- Speaks more when working with his hands.
    

### Hollis Initial Information

Hollis can explain:

- He cannot leave. The fog always brings him back.
    
- The rats move with intention and retreat when hurt.
    
- Sometimes the rats speak with voices that do not belong to them.
    
- Lit, barricaded rooms stay safer.
    
- The inn seems to recognize occupied rooms.
    
- The candle in the window was already burning when he found this refuge.
    
- He knows the old rule that one light means occupied.
    
- He does not know who or what lit this particular candle.
    
- He has seen the candle relight without anyone touching it.
    
- He does not know Eeler is still inside.
    
- He assumes Eeler is dead, missing, or was never here when the haunting began.
    

### Hollis Lines

> “Don’t lean on that table. It’s doing more work than it looks.”

> “Give me half a minute and something heavy. I can make that doorway harder to use.”

> “I don’t need to understand the inn to know which parts are holding.”

> “That candle brought me in. I’m still deciding how I feel about that.”

> “If you hear my voice from the wrong side of a wall, ask me something only I’d complain about.”

## Eeler

Back to: [[Session 3 — Eeler’s Inn Progression#Scene 16 Eeler Awakens|Scene 16]]

### Spotlight

Eeler is the innkeeper who became bound to his own inn when its hospitality magic tried to save him.

He is the heart of the hub. Rescuing him turns Eeler’s Inn from a haunted location into a living refuge.

### Fill

Eeler wakes as a skeletal lich with weak control over the inn. He does not need sleep, food, or warmth, but he still thinks in terms of rooms, names, lamps, meals, keys, and guests.

His memory is damaged around his death. He understands that the inn preserved him badly. He does not yet know how many people were harmed by the malfunctioning magic.

### Edge

Eeler solves problems through hospitality and house authority.

He names rooms, assigns beds, opens proper doors, closes unsafe ones, updates the ledger, relights lamps, and treats supernatural boundaries as house rules. When danger threatens the inn, he tries to define who belongs inside and what must leave.

His flaw is that he wants the inn to be a refuge again before he fully understands what it has become. He may overpromise safety because being a host is the part of himself he still recognizes most clearly.

### Practical

Eeler created or inherited the old hospitality rite tied to the inn.

Its guiding promise was simple: no soul beneath this roof should be abandoned. That promise became dangerous when Eeler lay dying in the master suite and the inn tried to keep him.

His service bell is the practical symbol of his failure. He rang it when he needed help, and no one came.

### Motivation

Eeler wants to make the inn safe enough to deserve guests again.

He feels immediate gratitude toward Gib, but his longer motivation is repair. The building, the ledger, the dead, the rats, the rooms, and the road custom all became tangled through him. He wants to put those things in order.

### Table Mannerisms

- Calls people “guest,” “friend of the house,” or “keyholder” depending on what they have earned.
    
- Notices practical comfort first: whether someone is wet, hungry, injured, cold, or standing too long.
    
- Touches doorframes, keys, or lamps while thinking.
    
- Apologizes for the state of the inn in specific terms rather than broad guilt.
    
- Avoids discussing his death directly until trust deepens.
    

### Eeler Lines

> “You are injured. Sit down. Gratitude can wait until you are less likely to fall over.”

> “I remember the bell. I remember reaching for it. After that, the inn remembers more than I do.”

> “This house was meant to keep people from being abandoned. It has done that badly. I intend to correct it.”

> “If you bring someone here, give me their name before the inn takes it for itself.”

> “I cannot promise the whole building is safe. I can promise I will notice when it changes.”

# Room Claiming

Back to: [[Session 3 — Eeler’s Inn Progression#Scene 4 First-Floor Objective|Scene 4]]

A room becomes claimed if Gib and Hollis can do most of the following:

- Light it
    
- Clear immediate threats
    
- Block major rat paths or doors
    
- Establish a physical connection to the current safe area
    
- Prevent the room’s haunt or spirit from immediately resetting
    

Claimed rooms are safe enough for Hollis to move through and reinforce.

Each reclaimed area should provide one of the following:

- A useful mundane item
    
- A clue about the inn
    
- A better barricade position
    
- A safe place to Treat Wounds
    
- A path toward the stairs
    
- An advantage against the possessed rats

# Creature Use Guide

## Running the Possessed Rats

Back to: [[Session 3 — Eeler’s Inn Progression#Scene 5 Bar and Kitchen|Scene 5]]

The possessed rats are part of the same infestation.

During early skirmishes, they retreat after one of the following happens:

- Gib deals a meaningful amount of damage.
    
- Gib kills or disables one of the possessed rats.
    
- Gib protects the light or barricade they were attacking.
    
- Gib uses the environment cleverly.
    
- The rats successfully drag an object deeper into the inn.
    

The rats usually retreat toward the next room, the stairs, or the nest.

# Haunt Rules Quick Reference

Back to: [[Session 3 — Eeler’s Inn Progression#Scene 3 First Haunt — Phantom Footsteps|First Haunt]]

## What Is a Haunt?

A haunt is a spiritual hazard. It usually forms from the emotional or spiritual imprint of a death, tragedy, or unresolved event.

In Eeler’s Inn, haunts are symptoms of the inn’s unstable spiritual state. They do not mean Eeler is evil.

## Detecting Haunts

A haunt lists a **Stealth DC**.

- If Gib is Searching, use the normal exploration rules.
    
- If the haunt’s signs are obvious from the scene, let him notice the warning signs without a roll.
    
- If Gib gains **Haunt Ingenuity**, roll secret checks for him to notice haunts even when he is not actively Searching.
    

## Disabling Haunts

A haunt lists the skills and DCs that can disable it.

For Eeler’s Inn, also allow appropriate story-based methods when they match the haunt’s theme.

Examples:

- Reading names from the guest ledger
    
- Lighting a room properly
    
- Acknowledging a dead guest
    
- Restoring a threshold
    
- Returning stolen objects to the correct room
    
- Separating Eeler’s influence from foreign spirits
    

## Critical Failure

If Gib critically fails a check to disable a haunt, the haunt triggers.

## Reset

Each haunt has a reset entry.

For this session, separate temporary suppression from permanent settling:

**Triggered or endured:** the haunt goes quiet until its reset time.

**Disabled:** the room becomes safe long enough for Hollis to move the barricade and claim the area.

**Settled through story:** the haunt does not return unless the inn becomes unstable again later.

## Simple Hazards

All three listed haunts are simple hazards. A simple hazard usually has one reaction or effect when triggered, then resets later if not fully settled.

# Seventh Witness Reflective Glass Reveal

Back to: [[Session 3 — Eeler’s Inn Progression#Scene 13 Seventh Witness Reveal — Reflective Glass|Scene 13]]

The Seventh Witness does not gain Witnessed Echoes this session.

This session reveals more about the mirror’s existing 0 HP transformation effect.

## Known Effect

When a creature is brought to 0 HP while Gib is invested in the Seventh Witness, the creature seemingly shatters into shards of reflective glass, showing the creature’s final moments.

## Session 3 Development

Because the rats are possessed, the glass remnants may show more than the rat’s final physical moment.

They can briefly reveal:

- The spirit riding inside the rat
    
- A frightened humanoid face reflected inside the glass
    
- Eeler’s golden light tangled with foreign spirits
    
- The nest’s connection to the possessed rats
    
- The master key reflected from an angle Gib could not physically see
    

## Limits

This does not grant a new active ability.

The mirror is not replaying the past generally. It is showing distorted final impressions through creatures affected by its existing glass transformation.

The information should be brief, unsettling, and tied to creatures or nest fragments transformed by the Seventh Witness.

## The Key to Eeler's Inn

Eeler gives this to Gib after being rescued.

The key anchors Gib to Eeler’s Inn and can redirect certain extradimensional or reflection-based travel effects to the inn instead of their original destination.

If circumstances would prevent Gib’s body from traveling, only his soul travels to Eeler’s Inn while his body remains where it would have been. His body is unconscious while separated, and his soul appears inside the inn as a visible spiritual reflection of himself.

This creates an emergency escape option with serious consequences.

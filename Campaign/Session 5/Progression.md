# Recap

Gib cleared the second floor of Eeler’s Inn, discovered the truth behind Squibbles’s golden eyes, and restored Eeler’s body in the master suite. Eeler awakened as an undead skeleton with golden light in his sockets, his memories incomplete but returning. Hollis remained downstairs, sick, exhausted, and unsure of his own past. # Opening Scene

## Read Aloud

Cold stone presses against your left palm.

The tunnel is narrow enough that your shoulder nearly brushes the opposite wall. Water has gathered between the fitted blocks beneath your boots, carrying the smell of wet limestone, lamp smoke, and old metal.
Your tongue finds the edge of one lower tusk.

You taste blood.

You have been biting the inside of your cheek again.

A hooded lantern hangs from your right hand. Its light passes over walls marked by old tools and older claws. Some of the carvings have been worn shallow by water. Others remain sharp enough to catch the light.

You recognize them.

You recognize this tunnel.

You know the shallow turn behind you. You know where the floor rises ahead. You know that after thirty-seven more steps, the passage should divide around a stone support carved with three intersecting circles.

Your spectacles slip down your nose.

You catch them at the bridge with one finger and push them back into place. Your hand remains there for a moment. Thumb and forefinger roll the thin arm of the frame back and forth while you stare ahead.

Something is wrong.

The passage does not divide.

A wall stands where the tunnel should continue.

It has not collapsed. There is no loose stone, no shattered support, and no debris beneath it. The masonry is fitted as cleanly as the rest of the passage, pale blocks running uninterrupted from floor to ceiling.

You raise the lantern.

There is a seam in the center.

It is finer than a knife cut and too straight to be natural.

You place two fingers against it.

The stone is colder than the surrounding wall. The chill passes through the pads of your fingers, along your wrist, and into the bones of your forearm.

Something moves on the other side.

Not close enough to touch the wall.

Close enough that you hear stone scrape against stone.

Your fingers return to your spectacles. You turn the arm of the frame once, then again.

You are certain of one thing.

This wall was not here yesterday.

## Treat Disease Hollis
- Hollis is feverish, weak, and mentally fogged.
- Success: he looks less gray and can hold a conversation more steadily.
- Failure: he remains shaky, irritable, and slow to recall details.

## Treat Disease Gib
- Reinforce that the rat bites mattered.
- Success: he stabilizes and can focus on the conversation.
- Failure: he still has to push through discomfort and fatigue.

## What Eeler can tell Gib about Molak
- Eeler knew Molak in Jaha.
- Molak lived in Jaha for most of his life.
- Eeler remembers him as thoughtful, capable, and increasingly difficult to fully understand.
- Eeler cannot yet explain the full change, only that the Molak he remembers and the Molak he later came to fear do not feel like the same man.
- Eeler believes Jaha is the correct place to start if Gib wants answers.

### If Gib presses
- Eeler remembers courts, scholars, old halls, and routes in Jaha more clearly than regions or modern politics.
- He may recall specific feelings before specific facts.
- He is more certain about Molak being tied to Jaha than about exact dates.

## What Eeler can tell Gib about the Inn
- Eeler only partly understands it.
- He can feel that the inn listens to him more now.
- It can clean itself, stir itself, and obey simple acts of will.
- He suspects it has become tied to roads, thresholds, and places of arrival.
- He does not know whether the inn is traveling, manifesting, or revealing doors to where it ought to be.
- He believes strong memory and intention may help anchor it.

### Eeler's phrasing
- “I owned an inn. I do not know when it became more inn than building.”
- “It feels like it has been waiting for me longer than I’ve been dead.”
- “I think it wants to be where travelers are. Or where they ought to be.”

## What Hollis can tell Gib
- Dena Rook wanted to leave.
- She knew more than Hollis understood at the time.
- She was the one who left the map at Knight’s Refuge.
- Hollis remembers her as urgent, frightened, and frustratingly certain.
- He cannot say who she really is or how she knew what she knew.
- He may admit that part of him thinks she was trying to help, while another part resents that she left him behind.

### If Gib asks directly who Dena is
- Hollis does not know, or cannot trust what he remembers.
- Best answer: “She knew things she shouldn’t have. And she acted like there wasn’t time to explain.”

## Where to next?
- Eeler points toward Jaha.
- Hollis has no better answer.
- Jaha is where Molak’s life becomes a real trail instead of a rumor.

# Eeler's Inn Manifests in Jaha

Use this when the inn arrives along the river between Jaha proper and the western expansion settlement.

Fog comes first.

It slides low over the water in a pale sheet, swallowing the muddy bank and the posts of the river landings. The Olongo goes quiet under it. Then, through the mist, the outline of the inn begins to assert itself: dark rooflines, shuttered windows, the suggestion of balconies and eaves where a moment ago there had only been riverbank.

Lanterns inside the inn kindle one by one.

The building stands where ancient stone gives way to newer timber construction, poised between two different Jahas. On one side lies the older city across the water, terraces and monumental walls rising behind the riverside districts. On the other stands the western settlement, more practical and crowded, with docks, storage sheds, patched roofs, and the muddy life of a place still being built.

The inn does not crash into the world. It settles into it with eerie confidence, as if it has every right to be there.

Its reflection in the river is wrong for a moment. The windows look deeper below the water than above it, and the lamplight trembles in long, thin gold lines before the current breaks them apart.

A few river workers stop what they are doing. Someone on the dock lowers a crate. A dog begins barking, then whines and backs away. The inn stands complete, lit, and waiting, with its front facing the flow of travelers as if it had always expected them.

## GM note
- This should feel uncanny but not hostile.
- It is a threshold moment.
- The inn is not attacking the city. It is arriving.

# Touchdown in Jaha

Gib’s first impression of Jaha should be that it is enormous, inhabited, and unfinished all at once.

Across the river, lights scatter through terraces, streets, and towers in layered bands. Some districts are bright and orderly. Others fade into dark overgrowth, scaffolds, rope lines, and half-restored facades. The city feels awake in the night. Lamps are being lit. Voices carry over water. Boats move slowly between landing points. Above it all, the great structures of old Jaha loom with the stubborn weight of a place built for more people than currently live there.

This is not a dead ruin. It is a city trying to live inside one.

# Jaha Key Figures Mashup

Use this as a montage once Gib reaches Jaha or as a transition scene before he starts interacting with the city directly.

### Iakanne
Beneath the Kehwamesi, Iakanne sits at a long table layered with petitions, reports, and draft orders. An aide reads aloud the newest assessment from West Terrace: one support needs repair soon. Without looking up, Iakanne signs an extension allowing the families to remain, then reaches for the next document before the ink is dry.

### Barashzi
At West Terrace, Barashzi crouches with damp clay darkening their claws and forearms. Lantern light catches on unearthed stone and the edge of a communal floor pulled back just enough to show what lies beneath. An engineer waits nearby, frustrated. Barashzi does not even look at him until the current layer of recovery is complete.

### Versenne
In a well-used office near the heart of the city, Versenne breaks the seal on another letter from the foreign surveying institution. The message is brief and unfriendly. Aides come and go around her with ledgers, permits, and route requests. She sets the letter aside only long enough to promise access to someone else.

### Oakuma
In Star-Crossed Charts, Oakuma leans over a broad map weighted with stone samples and scraps of notation. Kajeera is arguing with her. Inali is stitching a hazard revision into a woven chart. Oakuma marks another route in fresh ink and says, without looking up, “If they wanted it quiet, they should have warned the people first.”

## GM note
- End the mashup by returning to Gib.
- The point is to establish motion before he picks a direction.

# Optional End Beat

If you want to end the session on arrival rather than getting deep into Jaha, end with the inn settled on the river and the city spread out in front of Gib.

Possible closing line:

Jaha is awake across the water, full of light, argument, memory, and unfinished business. Somewhere inside it, Molak’s life still left a shape behind.

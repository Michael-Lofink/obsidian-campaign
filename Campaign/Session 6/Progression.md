# Informal Recap
[[Campaign/Session 6/Progression#Table of Contents|Back to Table Of Contents]]

<u>*The Hidden Library* by Joshua Kyan Aalampour</u>

Read aloud:

- Last session, Gib restored the body of Eeler, the long-dead proprietor of Eeler’s Inn. Eeler awakened as a skeleton with golden light burning in his empty eye sockets, his scattered memories slowly returning.
- Gib treated the disease carried by the inn’s rats, curing himself and setting Hollis on course to recover. Hollis remained at the inn to rest.
- Eeler discovered that the inn itself had become his phylactery. Its rooms exist somewhere between physical places, allowing the building and its occupants to move in ways Eeler does not yet understand.
- Eeler remembered that his inn originally stood along the Olongo River outside Jaha. He also remembered Molak as a dromaar astrologer who lived in Jaha more than two centuries ago.
- Gib told Eeler about his birth from the mirror, Molak’s destruction of his village, and the strange dreams that seem connected to Molak. Eeler explained that dreams connect to a real plane of existence, though he could offer few answers about Gib’s experiences.
- Eeler returned the inn to the material world along the Olongo River and gave Gib a magical key capable of guiding him back through certain forms of supernatural travel.
- As Gib prepared to leave, Squibbles chose to follow him and became his permanent companion.
- Gib stepped outside just after midnight on 6 Pharast. Across the river, the pale terraces of Jaha were waking beneath the full moon.

# Scenes

## Reaction to [[Eeler's Inn]]
[[Campaign/Session 6/Progression#Table of Contents|Back to Table Of Contents]]

The western-expansion port-reeve received a fisherman’s report of lit windows beside an old landing where no occupied building had stood earlier that night. He has come to identify the structure, determine whether it presents a danger to river traffic, and decide whether it falls under the authority of the port office.

### He wants:
- To identify the owner or responsible occupant.
- To determine whether the structure is safe.
- To classify the inn as a residence, business, lodging house, riverside structure, or magical hazard.
- To return to his office quickly.
- To keep the matter within the port office.

### He will try to:
- Overlook minor irregularities.
- Treat the inn as an overlooked or previously unregistered structure.
- Inspect the exterior, landing, and publicly accessible rooms.
- Obtain Gib’s cooperation through routine questions.
- Leave with enough information to close or defer the report.

### He will avoid:
- Escalating the situation while everyone remains cooperative.
- Entering any room he considers dangerous.
- Making claims about magical phenomena he cannot verify.
- Involving public works, the river watch, restoration officials, or other offices unless the situation exceeds his authority.

### He knows:
- Eeler’s Inn does not appear on the current port survey.
- The old landing beside the inn was previously recorded as unused.
- Ordinary visitors may enter Jaha’s public districts.
- A functioning lodging house must eventually be registered.
- Several government offices currently dispute jurisdiction over ruins, property, excavation sites, and historical records.
	- See [[Jaha#Political Situation]]
	- See [[Jaha#Political Factions]]
- The public reputations of Iakanne, Barashzi, Versenne, and Oakuma.

### He does not know:
- Who Eeler is.
- That the inn is Eeler’s phylactery.
- How or when the inn arrived.
- Whether the inn is physically stable.
- Who Molak is.
- Why Gib has come to Jaha.

### If Gib cooperates:
- The port-reeve records Eeler’s Inn as an unverified preexisting riverside structure pending routine inspection.
- He identifies Gib or Eeler as the responsible occupant.
- He gives Gib directions to the port office, the Western Annex, the nearest bridge, and Jhebyiti.
- He warns that operating the inn as a business will require registration.

### If the port-reeve believes the inn is dangerous:
- He withdraws from the property.
- He closes the old landing and boardwalk to ordinary traffic.
- He sends for the river watch.
- He files the structure as a suspected magical hazard.

## In the Western Annex
[[Campaign/Session 6/Progression#Table of Contents|Back to Table Of Contents]]

### Scene Purpose
- Introduce Jaha’s ordinary nighttime activity.
- Allow Gib to orient himself and ask questions.
- Establish where different kinds of historical information are held.
- Introduce Shora Mujafor and Star-Crossed Charts.
- Give Gib a practical lead concerning Molak.

### Read Aloud

>The boardwalk from Eeler’s Inn meets the western bank at the end of a narrow muddy lane, and the Western Annex begins almost immediately. Warehouses and workshops crowd the rising ground ahead, assembled from timber, canvas, river stone, and pale blocks carried out from the ancient city. Lanterns hang from ropes stretched between buildings, while blue-green fungi glow from shallow beds beneath the eaves, lighting carts, stacked crates, and rain-darkened signs.
>
>A few hundred feet north, a broad bridge crosses the Olongo. Its lamps continue along a raised approach toward one of Jaha’s western gates, where guards, laborers, and loaded carts move in both directions. Across the river, storehouses, work yards, gardens, and scattered buildings line the road toward the city. Jaha’s immense walls rise behind them, with the upper tiers of the Kehwamesi visible farther within.
>
>Behind you, Eeler’s Inn leans over the water from an old landing just south of the bridge. Its warm windows join the other lights trembling across the river, and from this distance it appears to have occupied the waterfront for years.
>
>The Annex is already deep into its working day. Dockhands unload sacks of grain and crates of excavation tools from low riverboats. Hammers sound from an open workshop where a damaged pulley is being repaired. Expedition crews gather around painted route boards, checking packs and counting coils of rope before leaving for the terraces. Vendors pour dark coffee from clay pots and serve bowls of spiced vegetables, river fish, and hot grain to workers beginning their shifts.
>
>Humans and iruxis move through the same crowded lanes alongside foreign scholars, merchants, guards, and laborers. Some wear clean clothes and carry sealed permits. Others are covered in clay, dust, or river mud. Conversations in Mwangi, Iruxi, and Lirgeni overlap beneath the hammering of workshops and the rumble of carts.
>
>A public notice board stands where the muddy lane meets the nearest street, crowded with route closures, employment offers, missing-person notices, and government orders. A few yards beyond it, a young woman has spread a river map across the top of an empty crate. One hand holds the parchment flat while the other traces the western bank.
>
>An older fisherman stands across from her, turning his cap between both hands. “I know where I was,” he insists. “I’ve worked that bend for twenty-three years.”
>
>“Then show me where the windows were,” the woman replies.
>
>He points once, hesitates, and moves his finger farther south. She studies the mark, checks the bridge and river against the map, and looks toward Eeler’s Inn standing plainly above the water where her parchment shows an unused landing and an empty stretch of bank.

### Things That Can Be Stumbled Into
- **Shora Mujafor:** She is questioning the fisherman who reported Eeler’s Inn and comparing his account against a current river map.
- **Public notice board:** Contains West Terrace notices, route closures, expedition work, Moon Fortress warnings, missing-person reports, and waystone restrictions.
- **Night food stalls:** Provide food, magical coffee, rumors, and basic information about the city.
- **Expedition outfitters:** Workers discuss Versenne’s foreign partnerships and the equipment dispute involving West Terrace.
- **Restoration workers:** An iruxi crew returning by boat discusses a site placed under Barashzi’s authority.
- **Port office:** Holds current river surveys, dock registrations, and basic property records.

### Shora’s Current Activity

Shora came to the river after one line in Inali’s woven chart shifted toward the Western Annex. She found the fisherman who reported seeing lights beside the unused landing and is trying to establish exactly what he witnessed.

She wants:
- An exact location and time.
- A description of what changed.
- To determine whether the fisherman saw the inn arrive or noticed it afterward.
- To compare his account against Inali’s chart and the current river survey.
- To return to Star-Crossed Charts with information Oakuma can use.

She will try to:
- Keep the fisherman focused on observable details.
- Establish where his boat was when he noticed the inn.
- Compare the inn’s position to the bridge, landing, and nearby buildings.
- Learn who currently occupies the structure.
- Arrange for Oakuma to question Eeler if his memories involve old Jahan locations.

She knows:
- Inali’s chart shifted shortly after midnight.
- The changed line points toward this section of the river.
- The current river survey shows an unused landing where the inn now stands.
- Oakuma keeps discontinued maps and records of altered routes.
- Public districts are open to visitors.
- Historical archives, ancestral testimony, excavation records, and restricted maps are controlled by different institutions.

She does not know:
- Who Eeler is.
- That the inn is Eeler’s phylactery.
- Who Molak is.
- Whether the chart reacted to the inn, Eeler, Gib, or something within the structure.
- How the inn arrived.

The fisherman:
- Is certain the inn was absent during his earlier journey upriver.
- Saw its illuminated windows while returning downstream.
- Saw no movement, collapse, splash, or magical display.
- Is becoming uncertain as Shora presses him for exact distances and times.
- Worries that the story will make him appear drunk or confused.

Shora keeps her attention on the fisherman until their exchange ends, Gib interrupts, or the fisherman identifies Gib as someone who came from the inn.

### Things That Require Asking
- Where civic and property records are stored.
- Where old astrological records are held.
- Who controls records concerning the pre-Jahan iruxi court.
- How visitors gain access to restricted records or excavation sites.
- Whether anyone recognizes Eeler’s name.
- Whether anyone recognizes Molak’s name.
- How to reach Jhebyiti, the Kehwamesi, or Star-Crossed Charts.

### Things That Can Only Be Discovered
- Inali’s woven chart shifted toward the river at approximately the same time Eeler’s Inn appeared.
- No current river map shows the inn at this location.
- The records held at Star-Crossed Charts also contain no previous structure matching the inn.
- Molak’s apparent age places the investigation within a disputed historical period involving pre-Jahan iruxi history.
- Information about Molak may be divided among civic archives, ancestral testimony, astrological records, and excavation reports.
- Shora can arrange for Oakuma to meet Gib at Star-Crossed Charts.
- Shora can ask Oakuma to visit Eeler’s Inn if Eeler cannot leave it.
- Oakuma will want Eeler to identify remembered locations directly on a map.
- Access to protected records requires cooperation from the institution controlling them.

### Possible Scene Endpoints
- Gib agrees to bring Eeler’s ledger to Star-Crossed Charts.
- Shora agrees to bring Oakuma to Eeler’s Inn.
- Gib decides which type of record he wants to pursue first.
- Gib receives directions into Jhebyiti and the public areas of the old city.
- Gib reaches the crossing into Jaha with a contact name and a clear destination.

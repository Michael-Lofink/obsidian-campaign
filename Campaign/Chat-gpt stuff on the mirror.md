### Gib
Gib is currently best understood as:

**Big’s reflected face, Sivanah’s immune response, Molak’s target-pattern, and his own emerging personhood.**

He has no reflection because he is made from reflection-stuff. Mirrors do not know whether to display him, receive him, or report him back to Sivanah.

His back does not exist, or cannot be properly perceived, because he was created from a front-facing reflection and separated before full embodiment. Symbolically, his front faces the mortal world, while his back still faces Sivanah.

That is why people’s minds slide off the details of his back.

That is why he feels pitiable rather than threatening.

That is why strangers rationalize their discomfort as “maybe his skin is toxic.”

That is why he is paranoid: he is psychologically traumatized, copied from Big, and also carrying immune-response instincts that tell him Molak is a lethal infection.

His current life matters enormously. His locks, go-bag, emergency fund, labor job, coworkers, and fear of being found are not filler. They are the proof that he is living as an individual rather than merely performing a biological function. The notes about his paranoia, lack of reflection, nonexistent/perception-filtered back, and cautious social presence are all central clues rather than random traits.

### Big
He is a 25-year-old thaumaturge tripkee, greedy, relic-focused, anxious, scrawny, and more interested in selling old objects than understanding them. He found the mirror and tried to sell it to Molak without researching it. That mistake created the conditions for Gib’s birth.

Big gave Gib:
His face.
His copied memories.
His thaumaturge instincts.
His relic knowledge.
Some survival habits.
Some selfishness and fear.

But Big did not give Gib everything.

Your notes already establish that Gib has developed traits Big did not really have, especially his uncertain desire to protect smaller or defenseless people and his coworkers. That is one of the clearest signs that Gib is becoming distinct from Big.

Big represents Gib’s copied past.

Molak represents Gib’s possible corrupted future.

Sivanah represents Gib’s origin-function.

Gib has to become something none of them define.

### Molak
Molak is a rogue reflection/aspect of Sivanah who became cancerous.

He was originally the reflection of a human dromaar. That dromaar identity became his dominant ego, which is why Molak presents as a large, imposing human dromaar with authority, lackeys, and physical intimidation.

Over time, Molak absorbed other reflections, secrets, or identity-fragments. He now contains multiple consciousnesses inside himself, but the dominant dromaar ego suppresses and organizes them.

He is not merely one escaped reflection.

He is a malignant cluster.

He can reproduce, assimilate, and spread. If he reconnects properly with Sivanah’s main body, he could theoretically corrupt the mirror-world itself and turn the whole organism cancerous.

That is why he is so dangerous.

Molak’s current motive is not fully locked, but the strongest version is:

**Molak wants the mirror because it is a major connection point to Sivanah’s body. He can use it to sever himself permanently, repair himself, assimilate more of Sivanah, or begin metastasis into the mirror-world.**

He knew about the mirror because he is intrinsically linked to Sivanah and the mirror-body.

### Sivanah
Sivanah is not a god in this campaign.

Sivanah is the entire mirror-world: one vast living organism made of reflections, veils, secrets, mirror-fauna, hidden selves, and reflected identities. Mortals may misinterpret her as a deity, queen, spirit, or planar ruler because they cannot easily understand an entire realm as one living being.

She does not think like a human, tripkee, or dromaar. She thinks like a body.

Reflections are sensory cells.

Veils are membranes.

Secrets are protected tissue.

Mirror-fauna are internal organisms.

Witnesses are immune or diagnostic cells.

The mirror Big found is one of her specialized organs.

Sivanah is not cruel, but she does not naturally understand individuality. To her, Gib is not initially “a person.” He is a detached immune response that successfully removed an infected organ from danger.

That is where the moral tension comes from.

### The Mirror
Sivanah is the living mirror-world, a vast organism made of reflections, secrets, veils, and mirror-fauna. Long ago, something wounded her from the material side, and that wound was bound into a physical object: a gold-rimmed mirror with a carved face on the back. The mirror is not designed to create beings. It is a sealed breach, a living scar, and a direct access point into Sivanah’s body.

Molak is a cancerous rogue reflection, originally organized around the dominant ego of a human dromaar. He contains multiple assimilated mirror-consciousnesses inside himself. He wanted this specific mirror because it would let him access Sivanah’s living body and begin spreading into it.

When Big tried to sell the mirror, Molak touched the wound. Big was touching it too, so Big’s reflection was caught in the surface. Molak’s cancerous essence made contact with Sivanah’s living tissue, and the wound reacted violently. It attempted to seal, reject, and isolate the malignancy. In that reaction, living mirror-substance was expelled through Big’s reflected shape.

That expelled substance became Gib.

Gib has Big’s face and memories because Big walas the available reflection-tempte. Gib is linked to Molak because Molak’s corruption triggered and contaminated his creation. Gib has no reflection because he is made of reflective tissue. His back does not properly exist because he was born from a front-facing image and still has one metaphysical side oriented toward Sivanah.

Gib began as a defensive reaction.

Gib is becoming a person.
